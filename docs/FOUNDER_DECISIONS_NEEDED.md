## Decision 1 — Entity State

**Question:** Which state do we form the LLC in?

**Considerations:**

- **Delaware** — Standard for tech. Strong contract law. Co-op
  conversion requires converting to a Delaware co-op or moving the
  entity.
- **Wyoming** — Cheap, no state income tax, strong privacy. Co-op
  statute thin.
- **Colorado** — Strong co-op statute. Home to many co-ops. Higher
  ongoing costs.
- **California** — Strong co-op law, home to many worker co-ops. Higher
  franchise tax.
- **Nevada** — No state income tax. Co-op statute thin. "Vegas" angle
  is on-brand but shouldn't drive the legal structure.

**Recommendation:** Delaware LLC now, plan to convert to a Colorado or
California co-op when the trigger hits. Most common path for tech-that-
wants-to-become-a-co-op. Confirm with a co-op lawyer.

## Decision 4 — Benefit Corporation as Public Off-Ramp

**Question:** Does "or a member-owned benefit corporation" stay in the
public covenant?

**Why it matters:**

- Grok Finding 1: benefit corp is not a co-op. Delaware public-benefit
  language still allows investor-owned equity, founder-controlled board,
  and a sale.
- "Member-owned" is undefined. "The community decide" has no vote
  mechanism before members exist.
- This is how you keep the branding and drop the ownership.

**Options:**

- **A:** Delete benefit corp from public covenant. Commit only to a
  cooperative. Cleanest story, less flexibility.
- **B:** Keep benefit corp as an option, require non-negotiable
  attributes attached. More flexible, more text.
- **C:** Remove from public page. Keep in Article X as legal fallback
  with attributes attached. Public story is clean, legal structure has
  locked fallback.

**Recommendation:** Option C.
