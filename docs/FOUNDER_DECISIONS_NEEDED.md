## Decision 1 — Entity State

**Question:** Which state do we form the LLC in?

**Considerations:**

- **Delaware** — Standard for tech. Strong contract law. Co-op conversion
  requires conversion to a Delaware co-op or moving the entity.
- **Wyoming** — Cheap, no state income tax, strong privacy. Co-op statute
  is thin.
- **Colorado** — Strong co-op statute. Home to many co-ops. Higher
  ongoing costs.
- **California** — Strong co-op law, home state to many worker co-ops.
  Higher franchise tax.
- **Nevada** — No state income tax. Co-op statute is thin. "Vegas" angle
  is on-brand but shouldn't drive the legal structure.

**Recommendation:** Delaware LLC now, with a plan to convert to a
Colorado or California co-op when the trigger hits. This is the most
common path for tech-that-wants-to-become-a-co-op. A co-op lawyer who
has done this specific conversion should confirm.

## Decision 4 — Benefit Corporation as Public Off-Ramp

**Question:** Does "or a member-owned benefit corporation" stay in the
public covenant?

**Why it matters:**

- Grok's Finding 1: a benefit corporation is not a co-op. Delaware
  public-benefit language still allows investor-owned equity, a
  founder-controlled board, and a sale.
- "Member-owned" is undefined. "The community decide" has no vote
  mechanism before members exist.
- This is how you keep the branding and drop the ownership.

**Options:**

- **A:** Delete benefit corp from public covenant. Commit only to a
  cooperative. Cleanest story, less flexibility.
- **B:** Keep benefit corp as an option, but require the non-negotiable
  attributes (one-member-one-vote, no investor veto, mission lock,
  capped founder interest). More flexible, more text.
- **C:** Delete from public page. Keep in Article X as a legal fallback
  with the attributes attached. Public story is clean, legal structure
  has an escape hatch that isn't visible to the community.

**Recommendation:** Option C. Public promise is a cooperative. Legal
fallback exists but is locked with the attributes. Say so honestly in
the covenant: "The public promise is a cooperative. If the legal
conversion requires an intermediate form, that form must carry the
same member-ownership and voting protections. There is no path to
investor-owned."
