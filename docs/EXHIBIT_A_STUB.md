# Exhibit A — Conversion Plan (Stub)

**Status:** Stub. Version 0.1. Subject to legal review.
**Published:** 2026-09-27
**Target full draft:** 2026-10-15
**Entity now:** Delaware LLC
**Entity at conversion:** Community-owned cooperative (Colorado or
California co-op preferred; final state TBD with counsel)

This is the one-page sketch the Trust Covenant promised. It is ugly on
purpose. It exists so the conversion is not a blank page.

---

## 1. Trigger

Conversion begins the day the first of these fires:

- 50,000 Registered Users with at least one Verified Contribution, or
- $500,000 in cumulative consolidated gross revenue, or
- 2029-12-31.

All three triggers are defined in Article X of the operating
agreement. The definitions cannot be amended without a two-thirds
member vote (or, before members exist, founder + two-thirds Advisory
Council).

---

## 2. Conversion Timeline

- **Day 0:** Trigger fires. Founder publishes a dated conversion
  notice.
- **Day 90:** Conversion documents filed with the state. Public.
- **Month 12:** Conversion closed. Members seated. Board elected.

Specific performance attaches to the Day-90 filing duty, not the
Month-12 close. If the filing doesn't happen, the Council or any
Member can sue to compel it.

---

## 3. The Entity

Public promise: a **cooperative corporation** with one-member-one-vote
governance.

Legal fallback (Article X): a **member-owned benefit corporation** is
permitted **only if** it carries all four non-negotiable attributes:

1. One-member-one-vote on ranking, ad, and data policy.
2. No investor veto over conversion.
3. Patronage distribution or mission lock.
4. Founder's economic interest capped by a published formula.

If any attribute is missing, the fallback is void.

---

## 4. Membership

- **Who:** Every registered user in good standing. Free.
- **Voting:** One member, one vote. Businesses with claimed listings
  are barred from voting on ranking-algorithm and ad-policy changes.
- **Terms:** 12-month rolling. Members can leave anytime. Data exports
  with them.
- **Governance:** Board of 5–7 elected by members. Annual elections.
  Staggered terms.

---

## 5. Founder's Economic Interest

Formula (draft, subject to counsel and tax review):

- Founder receives a capped buyout based on trailing 12-month revenue
  at the conversion date, paid over 36 months.
- Cap is set at a multiple published in the final Exhibit A.
- No equity in the co-op. No voting rights. No veto.
- Founder may be hired as an employee or serve on the board by member
  election. Not by right.

The specific multiple is the negotiation. The principle is: capped,
published, not perpetual.

---

## 6. Investor Equity or Debt

Any investor instrument must convert at the trigger to:

- A non-voting debt instrument with a fixed repayment schedule, or
- A formula buyout, paid over a period set in the original
  subscription agreement.

Investors sign an acknowledgment of these terms **before** any money
changes hands. Enforced by Article X.4.

---

## 7. Patronage Math

Two pools:

- **Reviewer pool:** Members who write reviews, claim listings, or
  complete help matches. 60% of surplus.
- **Payer pool:** Members whose activity drives revenue (bookings,
  lead conversions). 40% of surplus.

Percentages locked in final Exhibit A. Changing them requires the same
supermajority as amending Article X.

*(Note: this is a draft. Real patronage math depends on what actually
drives revenue. This is a starting sketch.)*

---

## 8. What Cannot Change

The following survive any conversion, any merger, any acquisition:

- One-member-one-vote on ranking, ad, and data policy.
- No sale of user data. Ever.
- No pay-to-play rankings.
- One-click cancellation. No dark patterns.
- Exportable data. Standard format.
- This Exhibit A. Any change requires a two-thirds member vote.

---

## 9. Acquisition

If TrailerVegas is ever acquired:

- The acquirer must assume Article X in writing.
- Asset sales, exclusive brand licenses, and acqui-hires that move
  community data are banned without the same supermajority.
- If the acquirer won't sign, the deal does not happen. The board has
  no discretion to override.
