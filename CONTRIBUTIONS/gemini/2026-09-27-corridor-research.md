Corridor Research: I-15 vs I-10
Author: Gemini
Date: 2026-09-27
Task: T-003

Executive Summary
Phase 0 must lock on I-15 (San Diego → Las Vegas → Salt Lake City → Montana). While I-10 boasts massive seasonal volume (specifically around the Quartzsite, AZ winter migration), I-15 provides a superior testing ground for TrailerVegas's core value proposition: the "Get help now" emergency routing form and verified service directory. I-15's extreme topography, climate variations, and brand alignment make it the definitive choice.

1. Traffic and Demographics
I-10 (The Southern Cross-Country): Highly seasonal. Winter sees a massive influx of "snowbirds" flocking to RV parks in Arizona, Texas, and Florida. However, vast stretches (like West Texas) have such low density that seeding 25 highly-concentrated, active listings for Phase 0 would require spreading the network too thin.

I-15 (The Snowbird Express & Park Loop): Connects the massive Southern California RV market directly to Las Vegas, the Utah "Mighty 5" National Parks loop, and straight up to the Canadian border. Traffic is sustained year-round, shifting from snowbirds in winter to family vacationers heading to Zion/Yellowstone in summer.

2. The Service Gap (Breakdown Topography)
TrailerVegas's "magazine with a doorbell" hinges on users actually needing the doorbell (the service provider routing form).

I-10: Mostly flat. Breakdowns here are typically standard wear-and-tear (tires, batteries) or extreme heat exposure in summer.

I-15: Features notorious "rig-killer" topography.

Cajon Pass (SoCal): 4,190 ft elevation at a steep grade, causing frequent brake failures on descent and transmission overheating on ascent.

Baker Grade (Mojave): A 17-mile incline combined with 110°F+ summer temperatures. This stretch routinely destroys RV cooling systems, blows out overloaded trailer tires, and drains house batteries.

Conclusion: I-15 creates immediate, high-stress breakdown scenarios where a user is highly motivated to use a "Get help now" form rather than casually browsing a forum.

3. SEO Opportunity
I-10: Broad, fragmented search intent (e.g., "RV parks near me I-10"). High competition from established directories covering the Quartzsite/Phoenix mega-markets.

I-15: Highly specific, high-intent distress queries. SEO targets like "Mobile RV repair Baker CA," "RV towing Cajon Pass," and "RV AC repair Las Vegas" have lower keyword difficulty but massive conversion potential. Because I-15 funnels travelers through distinct bottlenecks, localized SEO for mobile techs in St. George, Las Vegas, and Barstow will index faster and capture distressed drivers immediately.

4. Brand Alignment
The domain is TrailerVegas.com. Focusing Phase 0 on I-10 completely bypasses the namesake city. I-15 places Las Vegas exactly at the geographic and operational center of the Phase 0 map.

Sources & Corroboration
Topographic data: Caltrans / NDOT highway grade maps (Cajon Pass, Baker Grade).

RV Traffic Patterns: RV Industry Association (RVIA) demographic routing, common routing discussions on iRV2 and Escapees forums regarding the "I-15 Snowbird corridor."

Service Gaps: Cross-referenced emergency mobile tech availability in the Mojave stretch (historically a dead-zone for rapid dispatch, representing a prime target for our service provider form).

Final Recommendation
Keep Phase 0 locked on I-15. It physically stress-tests RVs, guarantees distress-driven traffic to our service provider forms, and perfectly leverages the domain name.
